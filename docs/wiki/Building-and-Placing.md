# Building and placing

There is no separate building mode. You pick something up from storage, put it down, and click it later to change it.

## Putting things down

1. Buy it (the online store delivers instantly) or find it in your storage.
2. Walk to your base (stand on or just beside your land), press `I`, find the row and press **Place**. Away from your base the button sets a waypoint to it instead.
3. A small **placing bar** shows what you hold. The storage list closes so the land is clear, and a grid appears. A
   green or red preview follows the pointer and says why a spot is not allowed.
4. Click to place. `R` rotates. `1` to `9` switch to another structure in storage. The **Storage (I)** button reopens the
   list. **Done** or `Esc` stops. It also stops by itself when you place the last one.

If your land is off screen when you start, the camera moves to it. Otherwise it stays where you put it.

### Lines

Drag to lay a line of single-tile structures (hedges, paths, spike traps, trenches, fences, walls, sandbag walls). Fences
and walls follow the grid line you start on and fill any tiles the pointer skipped.

### Fences and walls sit on grid lines

Fences, walls, sandbag walls and gates are placed **on the line between two tiles**, not in the middle of a tile. Corner
posts are shared where fences meet. A gate is two tiles long. Hedges stay inside tiles and join their neighbours. A fence
cannot run through the middle of a building.

### Fitting

Models are scaled to fit the tiles they cover. If a structure does not fit (outside your land, on top of something), the
preview turns red and the bar says why.

## The crew

A placed structure is a plan. A **crew of three** builds the oldest unfinished plans first, over game time. Each
structure has a build time (a picket fence 20 minutes, a family house 12 hours). While it is being built you see it
rising from the ground with the unfinished part as a blue blueprint. Percentages show on the card.

## The structure card

Click any structure you placed. The card shows:

- **Strength** and, for ponds, panels and glass, **Dirt** (see [Upkeep and repairs](Upkeep-and-Repairs.md)).
- **Level** (upgrades), **Working at** (output), and for barriers the hit points.
- Anything wrong with it: not connected to power or water, no seeds, worn out, dirty.

Buttons:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Rotate (`R`) | Turn it a quarter turn where it stands, around its middle. Instant and free. Tries the next turn if the first does not fit. |
| Move | Lift it and click where it should go. Instant and free. It keeps its id, strength, level, dirt and contents. `Esc` cancels. |
| Take down | Put it back in storage. A structure below 90% strength gives back scrap materials instead. |
| Repair, Upgrade, Clean | See [Upkeep and repairs](Upkeep-and-Repairs.md). |
| Show all cables and pipes | Turns the utility view on. |

A click on the ground right beside a fence counts as a click on the fence.

You must be at your base to rotate, move, take down or repair.

## Groups of structures

Fences, walls and gates; houses and shelters; wells and water; power; farming; defence; storage; utility; ponds, trees,
paths and garden features. About 75 structures in all. Each has a size, price, strength, build time and a picture made
from its real 3D model.

## Some structures to know

- **Pipe junction** and **power pole** join things up to 14 m apart.
- **Orchard plot** and **fish pond** make food without seeds.
- **Floodlight tower** lights the wall at night and needs power.
- **Watchtower**, **sandbag gun nest**, **spike trap**, **trench**: defence, ready for the zombies.
